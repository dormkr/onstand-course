# Week 30: Portfolio and case studies

**17–23 May 2027 · Block E · Outline**

## Why this week exists

Recruiters spend under a minute on a profile; engineers who interview you will spend 15 on whatever you link. Write for both: a one-screen summary for the first, and deep, honest case studies for the second. Everything you write must point at evidence (PRs, reports, recordings) that already exists. This week is mostly writing, not building.

## Tasks (15 h)

1. **Three case studies**, each 800–1,500 words, with a diagram and links to the evidence:
   - **"Benchmark first": YIN vs MPM in OnStand's tuner** (week 4, plus weeks 13 and 15): the decision, the data, what surprised you.
   - **Play to notation** (weeks 18–24): the pipeline, the error attribution, what's still wrong.
   - **An incident** (INC-0001 or INC-0002): what broke, how you found it, the systemic fix. Engineers love an honest incident story more than a feature list.
2. **The OnStand README v2** (engineering) and **a portfolio page** (one screen): who you are (a flutist who builds music software, Nairobi, EAT), three projects, three case studies, the open-source PRs, and a contact.
3. **CV v2:** every line backed by a number or a link. Your authorship story told honestly: "Launched OnStand with AI-assisted tooling; then re-engineered its core (tests, CI, monitoring, DSP benchmark) myself." That's true, it's what 2026 hiring managers want to hear, and it survives questioning.
4. **External review:** send the CV and portfolio to two working engineers (one in music tech if you can) with three specific questions. Make the changes and log what changed.
5. **Behavioural stories.** Write 6 STAR stories (situation, task, action, result) from the course and OnStand, using the [Tech Interview Handbook behavioural guide](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/): a conflict or disagreement, a failure, a time you were wrong, a time you dealt with ambiguity, a time you went deep, a time you shipped under pressure.

## Gate

- **w30.cases:** three case studies published.
- **w30.review:** CV and portfolio reviewed by two engineers, with changes made.
